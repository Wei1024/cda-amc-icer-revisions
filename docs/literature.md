# Literature: manufacturer vs agency ICERs, and ML/LLM on HTA documents

Searched 2026-11-12, with a second novelty search on 2026-11-15. Both were targeted web searches, not a systematic review. The two NICE papers closest to the novelty claims (Carroll 2017, Walton 2019) were then read in full. Items marked (unverified) came from a fetch tool's summary of the paper rather than verbatim text. Items marked (abstract only) were paywalled and read from the abstract or search summaries.

## Manufacturer vs reviewer ICERs (other agencies)
- **NICE, multiple technology appraisals.** Chauhan, Miners, Fischer. *Int J Technol Assess Health Care* 2007;23(1):96–100. [PDF](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/9701/1/IL4.pdf). In 32 appraisals, manufacturers' lower ICERs came mainly from **lower benefits estimated for the comparator** and **lower costs for their own technology**. This is the closest precedent for tracing where a gap comes from. The authors stress how hard it was to explain why the two analyses diverge.
- **NICE, reviewers' exploratory analyses.** Carroll, Kaltenthaler, Hill-McManus, et al. *Value Health* 2017;20(6):785–91. [abstract](https://www.ispor.org/publications/journals/value-in-health/abstract/Volume-20--Issue-6/The-Type-and-Impact-of-Evidence-Review-Group-Exploratory-Analyses-in-the-NICE-Single-Technology-Appraisal-Process); full text read.
  - **Sample:** 100 single technology appraisals, 2009–2014. 93 Evidence Review Group (ERG) reports contained exploratory analyses: 798 in all, a mean of 8.5 per report.
  - **Types:** fixing errors (35% of reports), addressing violations (18%), matters of judgement (89%), and a new ERG base case (48%).
  - **Influence:** they influenced 72% of consultation documents and 47% of final decisions.
  - **Analysis:** narrative synthesis only. The authors say the sample was too small for statistics.
  - **What it doesn't do:** it **counts the types of change and their influence on decisions, but never measures their effect on the ICER**. The quoted ERG reports do give the ICER effect of single changes, but these are not aggregated. Among future work, the authors list assessing the ICER impact of company errors and analysing the "matters of judgement" category in depth.
- **NICE, what predicts the reviewers' work.** Kaltenthaler, Carroll, Hill-McManus, et al. *PharmacoEcon Open* 2016;1:99–108 [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41669-016-0001-4), and the full report in *Health Technol Assess* 2016;20(26) (abstract only). Same 100 appraisals. It tested predictors of **how many** exploratory analyses an ERG runs, not of the ICER gap. The number was unrelated to disease area or the company's ICER, but differed by ERG.
- **NICE, size of the gap.** Walton, O'Connor, Carroll, Claxton, Hodgson. *PharmacoEcon Open* 2019;3:403–10 (online Jan 2019). [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41669-018-0113-0) (open access); full text read.
  - **Sample:** 146 single technology appraisals, 2010–2017.
  - **Size of the gap:** the ERG base-case ICER was a median **+32.7%** above the company's (mean +63%): +41.1% for advanced cancer and +22.5% for non-advanced cancer. The mean was £31.6k (company) vs £51.6k (ERG). Cases where the company claimed dominance were excluded, so the gap is understated.
  - **Regressions:** they **use the gap to predict the preliminary decision**: each £1,000 of difference lowers the odds of a positive decision by 0.04. **The size of the gap itself is only described by disease area, never modelled.**
  - **Price analogue:** a Patient Access Scheme discount introduced after the first decision cut the final ICER by a median of 38.7%.
- **Other critiques of submitted models,** cited in Carroll 2017, not read: Ghabri et al. *PharmacoEconomics* 2016 (France, uncertainty in the first 28 HAS submissions); Hill, Mitchell, Henry. *JAMA* 2000 (problems in Australian PBS submissions).
- **NICE, recent practice.** ABPI CONNIE review, October 2025. [ABPI](https://www.abpi.org.uk/publications/reviewing-implementation-in-practice-of-the-nice-health-technology-evaluation-manual-connie-october-2025/). The decision-making ICER was closer to the External Assessment Group's estimate than the company's in 46 of 78 topics (59%), rising from 55% to 70% between 2023/24 and 2024/25. It is an industry source.
- **Australia (PBAC).** Lybrand, Wonder. *Int J Technol Assess Health Care* 2020. [PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/50269F447E3F7EF96D0AF8A8F6E78AD5/S026646232000029Xa.pdf/analysis_of_pbac_submissions_and_outcomes_for_medicines_20102018.pdf). Submissions 2010–2018 needed 1.7 attempts on average. **The final agreed ICER is seldom published**, so a study like this one is much harder outside Canada.
- **Cross-agency.** *ClinicoEcon Outcomes Res* 2015. [Dove](https://www.dovepress.com/acceptance-of-health-technology-assessment-submissions-with-incrementa-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-CEOR). Among NICE, SMC, PBAC and CADTH submissions with ICERs above threshold, CADTH accepted none without restrictions.

**The comparison is useful.** NICE reviewers raise the ICER by about a third, while Canadian reviews roughly double it. Why Canada differs is a framing question for the write-up, not something this project can test.

## ML / LLM on HTA documents
- **LLM extraction:** Claude 3 Opus vs rule-based and classifier NLP on 50 NICE reports; 88–98% accuracy on 12/14 attributes, about 70% for outcome and comparator. [PMC13120885](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13120885/)
- **LLM extraction:** ISPOR Europe 2026, a custom GPT agent extracting cost-effectiveness models from 9 NICE/CADTH/ICER appraisals, in minutes rather than the 60–90 minutes an expert needs, with no hallucinated extractions reported; human verification still needed, especially for committee critiques and modelling assumptions. [ISPOR](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-europe-2026/artificial-intelligence-for-evidence-generation-and-synthesis/ready-for-prime-time-evaluating-large-language-models-for-automated-extraction-of-cost-effectiveness-models-from-hta-submissions)
- **LLM extraction:** a case study of LLM extraction of cost-effectiveness analysis data. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1098301525023691)
- **Prediction:** Janoudi, Rada, Yasinov, Richter. medRxiv 2026 preprint, Monte Carlo Committee Simulation. 14 LLM personas predict CDA-AMC **recommendation category and condition types**; n=67 (Oct 2024–Dec 2025). Accuracy 93.2% on confident cases vs a **91.8% majority-class baseline**. Does not predict ICER revisions or the size of price cuts. [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.02.26347434v2.full)
- **Prediction:** classical ML on HTA outcomes, all ISPOR abstracts with small samples, predicting recommendation categories:
  - multi-country, 550 outcomes [ISPOR 2024](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation/euro2024-4013/146240)
  - NICE/SMC/NCPE text classifiers [ISPOR 2025](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/presentations-database/presentation-cti/ispor-europe-2025/poster-session-5-2/predictive-algorithms-in-hta-what-factors-influence-decision-making-in-nice-smc-and-ncpe)

## Gap this project can fill
To our knowledge, from the searches above:

1. Every Canadian ICER-gap study covers a 2–7 year window and one stream, extracted by hand or from a commercial database. None covers 2020–2025 across oncology and non-oncology with one method.
2. **No study models the revision ratio as an outcome** or tests which submission characteristics predict it. Existing work stops at descriptive splits: by recommendation (Stino 2022), rare disease and clinical benefit (Balijepalli 2024), or advanced cancer (Walton 2019). Walton's regressions use the gap to predict NICE decisions, not the other way round. Kaltenthaler 2016 predicts how many analyses reviewers run, not the gap. Two papers explicitly call modelling the gap future work.
3. Critique types are tallied (Fashami 2024 in Canada; Carroll 2017 at NICE), and one small NICE study traced gaps to comparator benefits and technology costs (Chauhan 2007). **No study has quantified each type of change's contribution to the ICER across a full cohort.** Carroll 2017 lists part of this as future work. CDA-AMC's stepwise reanalysis tables make it possible.
4. **The cost vs QALY split is not new.** Saluja 2021 (pCODR oncology) and Chauhan 2007 (NICE) both did it. This project extends it to non-oncology drugs and to 2020–2025.
5. ML/LLM prediction work targets the recommendation category, which is about 90% "reimburse with conditions" and makes a weak target. Continuous economic outcomes are untouched.
6. Price reductions are described (Fashami 2024; Balijepalli 2024 links them to the revised ICER only loosely), but none of the studies above separates **how much of the cut the sponsor's own ICER already implied from how much the agency's revision added**.
7. **LLM extraction from HTA reports is not new as a method** (see above). What's new is using it on a full cohort for substantive analysis, validated by agreement between two independent models.
